## (a) H1 / H3 re-check — headline result

**H1 (fully funded) holds for all 11 shortlisted programs** on this fresh pass — no program that was previously marked fully-funded-for-internationals showed new evidence of a funding downgrade. The only things that shifted are **application-fee-waiver availability**, which is a different, much smaller-stakes screen than H1 and does **not** change any program's funding status:

- **FLAG — UC Berkeley (CPH)**: Berkeley Graduate Division fee waivers are explicitly restricted to "U.S. citizens or current permanent residents" and undocumented students; **"international applicants requiring an F-1 or J-1 visa when starting graduate studies are not eligible for a fee waiver."** **SOURCED** ([Berkeley fee waiver policy](https://grad.berkeley.edu/admissions/application-process/fee-waiver/); [undocumented-students page](https://grad.berkeley.edu/admissions/why-berkeley/prospective-undocumented-graduate-students/)). This applicant will pay the full $155 international fee. Does not affect H1 (the 5-year institutional funding guarantee for admitted students is unaffected and unrelated).
- **FLAG — MIT (EECS)**: MIT OGE fee waivers for international applicants require **currently attending a US college/university** and participation in a specific research-prep program (e.g. MIT CONVERGE, MSRP); pure financial hardship is explicitly not considered for non-US applicants. **SOURCED** ([OGE fee waiver page](https://oge.mit.edu/graduate-admissions/applications/application-fee-waiver/)). This applicant does not meet either criterion — no fee waiver expected via the central OGE route (a department-level EECS waiver was not found; worth one direct email to grad-ap@eecs.mit.edu, but budget it as UNCERTAIN/low probability).
- **RESOLVED — Columbia DBMI deadline**: official Vagelos instructions PDF dated **2026.08.24** confirms **1 Dec 2026, 5:00 PM EST**. The DBMI web page's "Dec. 1, 2027" is a typo — ignore. **Fee waiver update:** same PDF allows waiver for **financial need / personal hardship** (email BiomedicalSciences@cumc.columbia.edu before submit with docs) — broader than older "US citizens only" web notes. PDF filed at [`columbia-dbmi/materials/`](columbia-dbmi/materials/).
- No other program showed a funding red flag. Berkeley/UCSF CPH, Harvard AIM/GSAS, MIT EECS, Columbia GSAS, Mount Sinai, UW Allen, CMU MLD, Cornell, UPenn SEAS, BU CDS, and JHU CS all still show explicit, sourced "internationals included, no extra application needed" funding language for the admitted-student guarantee itself.

**H3 (admissible with MSc Data Science, Uppsala + BE Software Engineering, NUST, both English-taught, GRE 158V/164Q/4.0W) holds, no new problems found.** Nothing in this pass surfaced a hard degree-major requirement or a GRE floor that would newly fail this applicant. Confirmed/reconfirmed on this pass:
- Cornell CS **no longer considers the GRE at all** in PhD admissions (**SOURCED**, [Cornell CS FAQ via researchtweet.com summary](https://researchtweet.com/academic-jobs/fully-funded-phd-program-in-computer-science-at-cornell-university/) — matches prior deep-dive note).
- CMU MLD confirms GRE General is optional and explicitly does **not** issue English-proficiency waivers based on prior study in English, regardless of country — but this applicant would submit IELTS 8.5 directly, so this is a non-issue, not a new risk (**SOURCED** [MLD PhD page](https://ml.cmu.edu/academics/machine-learning-phd)).
- JHU CS: no GRE required (Whiting School policy) (**SOURCED** [PhD admissions](https://www.cs.jhu.edu/academic-programs/graduate-studies/phd-program/phd-program-admissions/)).
