# JHU Whiting / CS — Statement prompts (quoted from application portal)

**Source:** pasted from the live application materials (`Cover Letters/Inidivual Program Application Site Instructions/JHU CS (Whiting School).txt`), reconciled 2026-10-27. Portal: https://applygrad.jhu.edu/apply

## Statement of Purpose (required — ≤1,000 words)

> Please respond to the following prompt in no more than 1,000 words. Your statement should be well-organized, clearly written, and specific to your experiences and goals.
>
> Describe your primary areas of interest, related experiences, and motivations for pursuing a graduate degree at the Whiting School of Engineering at Johns Hopkins University.
> Explain the significance of your chosen field of study, how it aligns with your current professional goals, and how the program can help you achieve them.
> Highlight why you see yourself as a strong fit for the program, emphasizing relevant skills, experiences, and your vision for your future career.

## Personal Statement (optional — ≤1 page; pick ONE prompt)

> The Personal Statement is optional for all programs; check the instructions on your degree program website for specific criteria, if available. When included, this statement will help programs gain a more holistic understanding of you as an applicant.
>
> Provide a statement that is no more than one page in length, and please choose only one of the prompts below to respond to. Focus on the context of your decision to pursue a graduate degree.
>
> 1. Engineering the best solutions to complex problems is often achieved by drawing from the diverse ingenuity of people from different backgrounds, lived experiences, and identities. Johns Hopkins has long recognized the importance of enrolling a diverse student body. How will the life experiences that shaped who you are today enable you to contribute to JHU?
> 2. Describe an engineering problem that impacts your local community. This could be your school, neighborhood, town, region, or a group you identify with. Describe one to three things you might do as an engineer to solve the problem.
