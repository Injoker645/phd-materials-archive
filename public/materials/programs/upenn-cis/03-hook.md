## Keep (primary hook — still the best opener)

> When a doctor absorbs a hospital's unwritten habits over twenty years of practice and calls the result "clinical judgment," we trust it — no one ever asks her to prove which part of that judgment is real medicine and which part is just this particular hospital's history. When a model absorbs the same twenty years of the same hospital's habits from its notes, we call the result "bias" and demand it be explained before we trust it at all. Same information, same contamination, same inability — in either case — to cleanly separate the two. So what are we actually asking the model to do that we have never once asked the doctor to do — and is it a standard of evidence, or just a standard of who is allowed to be unaccountable?

**Why it still works at Penn:** AI-4-AI and IBI already sit at the junction of documentation, deployment, and clinical data. The hook lands as a *measurement* problem for applied clinical AI, which matches Johnson's deployment language and Eaton's transfer language better than a generic ethics opener.

## Softened variant (recommended for Penn statement)

> When a doctor absorbs a hospital's unwritten habits over twenty years and calls the result clinical judgement, we rarely ask which part is medicine and which part is that hospital's history. When a model absorbs the same habits from the same notes, we call it bias and demand an explanation before we trust the score. Same contamination, and in both cases we still lack a clean way to separate the two. I want to build that separation as a measurement on the model side, where it is actually testable across sites.
