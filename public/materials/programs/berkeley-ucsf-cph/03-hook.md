## Primary hook (provenance + asymmetrical accountability)

When a doctor absorbs a hospital's unwritten habits over twenty years and calls the result clinical judgment, we usually trust it. Nobody asks her, for a given decision, which part is medicine and which part is that hospital's documentation and discharge culture. When a model absorbs the same years of the same habits from notes and labels, we call it bias and demand an explanation before we trust the score at all. Same contamination. Same inability, on either side, to cleanly separate the two. So what are we actually asking the model to prove that we have never routinely asked the doctor to prove: a standard of evidence, or a standard of who is allowed to be unaccountable?

That is the question I want to spend a PhD on. Not another training trick that claims to remove site effects in aggregate (clinical multi-site benchmarks keep showing those often fail to beat simply pooling data). A measurement: for one prediction, how much of the score rests on site-invariant clinical signal versus site-specific process. If the split is wrong, it should fail a falsifiable cross-site check.

---

## Why this hook fits CPH specifically

- Obermeyer’s work already showed that a *convenient proxy* (cost) can look accurate while encoding structural inequity. Same shape of trap: the model optimises what the institution recorded, not necessarily what patients need.
- Chen’s programme is about auditing clinical ML under incomplete and shifted data. Provenance-as-measurement is an audit problem.
- CPH’s documented multi-site UC data and UCSF Health partnership are where you’d actually stress-test “does this evidence travel?”

Do not open with “Berkeley and UCSF are uniquely positioned…” Prestige flattery is banned in this voice.

## Secondary hook (underuse / omission) — optional second beat

If withholding a more accurate tool counts as a decision with consequences, why does deploying a flawed score feel like an action we’d have to answer for, while not deploying a better one often feels like nothing happened?

Use sparingly; primary hook already carries the thesis.

## Biographical on-ramp (after the hook, not before)

Keep short and factual:

I grew up in a hospital compound where my father worked. Later I wrote about how historical underrepresentation in medical research gets amplified when those records train models. On day one of my master’s I contacted a bioethics researcher at BMC Uppsala and went with them to an EU AI Act healthcare conference. The technical work that followed wasn’t clinical foundation models yet: *Cracks in the Feed* under the DSA, a Fyris ER demand model, a short KFSH CSICU dashboard data-dictionary role. Different domain for the thesis, but the habit is the same: treat data construction and failure modes as the research problem, not a preprocessing footnote.

Named gap: I don’t have a US academic EHR lab on my CV. That’s the honest gap. What I have is a clear measurement question and enough ML/statistics to start answering it inside a programme built for clinical deployment, not only for discovery.
