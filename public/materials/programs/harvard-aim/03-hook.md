## Primary hook (SoP-safe)

When a doctor absorbs a hospital's unwritten habits over twenty years of practice and calls the result clinical judgement, we trust it. When a model absorbs the same twenty years of the same hospital's habits from its notes, we call the result bias and demand it be explained. Overlapping sources of habit, and in either case no clean way to separate medicine from local routine. The difference is that one of those agents is actually auditable. I want to build that audit.

### Why this version for Harvard

- Same intellectual job as the NYU hook: converts generic algorithm aversion into **provenance**.
- Removes "who is allowed to be unaccountable" (sharp interview line; risky in a 1000-word SoP).
- Does not name NYUTron, Lang1, or any PI.
- Lands directly on AIM's stated equity / clinically impactful tools mission without prestige language.

### Hard twin (interview only, if the room can take it)

…So what are we actually asking the model to do that we have never once asked the doctor to do — and is it a standard of evidence, or just a standard of who is allowed to be unaccountable?

Use only verbally. Not in the written SoP unless he decides after a cold read that it still sounds like him.

## Secondary beat (optional, second paragraph or Personal Statement)

If withholding a more careful audit of a deployed score feels like nothing happened, while deploying a flawed score feels like an action we would have to answer for, that asymmetry is itself part of the problem I want to measure.

**Caveat:** underuse / omission-harm chain is the thinnest evidence base in the local deep-dive. Motivation only; no quantified "patients die because we skipped provenance" claim. Mark any numeric version **DO-NOT-USE** until verified.
