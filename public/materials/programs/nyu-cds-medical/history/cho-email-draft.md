## Body

Hi Kyunghyun,

Clinical AI is still treated as an optimisation problem. Better loss, bigger model, more notes. Your March post on Recht named what I'd been circling: we invent a proxy, call it the objective, and ignore the assumptions underneath. That's why NYU. There are people there who question the direction of the work, not just the optimiser.

I want these systems trustworthy as they enter hospitals. Interpretable models get written off as too inefficient to scale. Post-hoc XAI, including chain-of-thought, is unfaithful, and a 2025 review of XAI in clinical support found fewer than a third of papers involved a clinician. ML academics and health statisticians should not be the only ones signing off. Engineers need to be eloquent enough that an experienced physician can understand the system and cast doubt. Monthly meetings are not enough.

I've seen this from hospitals, a startup attempt, and corporate data. NYUTron is the shift that matters: full notes as the proxy for clinical decision support. Transfer will fail at the individual level, one surgeon's notes for a decade, not just hospital to hospital. Traceability should be a feature of that family, something a physician can interrogate, not a post-hoc story. Industry is burying its head in the sand. I hope NYU will not.

Applying to the '27 CDS medical track. I'd value a short conversation even if you aren't taking students.

All the best,
Moaaz Tameer
